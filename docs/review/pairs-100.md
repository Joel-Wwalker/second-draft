## 68. peat bog carbon

*mixed*

**BEFORE**

Peat bogs are unique ecosystems where partially decayed plant matter accumulates over thousands of years, resulting in the formation of peat—a highly carbon-rich material. These bogs typically develop in areas with acidic, waterlogged conditions, hindering decomposition processes.  The accumulation of plant material, particularly Sphagnum moss, contributes to the bogs' significant carbon sequestration capacity.  Unlike forests, peat bogs store carbon primarily in the form of partially decomposed organic matter rather than woody biomass.  The rate of carbon accumulation in peat bogs varies depending on factors such as climate, hydrology, and vegetation type—with colder, wetter climates generally promoting higher accumulation rates.  Peat deposits can reach impressive depths—sometimes several meters—and represent a substantial reservoir of stored carbon—a reservoir that is vulnerable to release through drainage, disturbance, or oxidation.
